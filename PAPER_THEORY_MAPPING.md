# Thesis Theoretical Structure and Mapping to the New Project

Source paper: *Research on Patient Healthcare-Seeking Decision-Making Based on Prospect Theory* (Wang Chenli).

## Core Theory of the Thesis

The thesis divides patient healthcare-seeking decision-making into two stages:

1. **Editing stage**: encode, merge, decompose, and delete information to form belief characteristics.
2. **Evaluation stage**: evaluate prospects based on subjective probability, reference points, and risk preference, then select the option with higher subjective value.

Prospect theory uses the value function `v(x)` and the weighting function `π(p)` to describe decisions. The value function defines gains and losses relative to a reference point, while the weighting function overestimates very small probabilities and underestimates moderate and large probabilities.

The main subjective systematic biases in the thesis are:

- Representativeness heuristic
- Availability heuristic
- Anchoring and adjustment
- Certainty effect
- Reflection effect
- Isolation effect

## Mapping from Thesis Variables to Colorectal Cancer Screening

| Thesis variables | Corresponding variables in colorectal cancer screening |
| --- | --- |
| Medical institution | Preferred hospital level |
| Timing of healthcare-seeking behavior | On-time screening, postponed screening, or temporary non-screening |
| Treatment method | Colonoscopy, other screening methods, or no screening |
| Belief structure | Disease cognition, health value account, health literacy, self-efficacy |
| Risk preference | Certainty effect, reflection and isolation effects, risk aversion |
| Disease cognition status | Risk perception, perceived severity, perceived benefits of screening, perceived barriers |
| Health values / psychological account | Cost reference point, subsidy sensitivity, health value account |
| Systematic bias | Representativeness, availability, anchoring, certainty, reflection, and isolation |
| Health literacy | HL1-HL3 |
| Quality of life | Self-rated health status and medical satisfaction |
| Self-management efficacy | 10 items from the General Self-Efficacy Scale |
| Age, sex, marital status, education | General demographic variables |
| Medical insurance status | Health insurance type and policy support |
| Economic burden of disease | Household income, reasonable cost threshold, self-payment sensitivity |
| Service accessibility | Family doctor, health promotion, medical satisfaction, and distance barriers |
| Delayed care-seeking | Delayed or unscreened status |

## Structural Equation Path for the New Project

```text
Health literacy, self-efficacy, disease cognition, service accessibility, policy support
        ↓
Belief structure

Representativeness heuristic, availability heuristic, anchoring and adjustment
        ↓
Risk preference and decision reference point

Belief structure + Risk preference + Decision reference point
        ↓
Screening intention

Screening intention + Service accessibility + Policy support - Systematic bias
        ↓
Actual colonoscopy screening behavior
```

## Differences from the Thesis

The thesis focused on patients with hypertension, and its outcomes were the timing of healthcare-seeking behavior, treatment method, and medical institution. This project focuses on high-risk colorectal cancer populations. The core decision is participation in colonoscopy screening, so the outcomes are reconstructed as:

- Screening intention over the next six months
- Actual colonoscopy participation
- Screening timing decision
- Hospital-level choice
- Screening method selection
