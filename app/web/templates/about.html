Prospect Theory-Based Research Design for Screening Decisions

From information cognition and editing to reference-point formation, risk preference, and final screening behavior
Animated theoretical map

One questionnaire, four linked layers of explanation

The study design follows the movement from objective context to cognitive editing, prospect-theoretic evaluation, and finally screening behaviour. Each layer maps back to questionnaire sections and analysable variables.

Theoretical core

Prospect theory uses the value function v(x) and the weighting function π(p) to describe decisions under uncertainty. Individuals evaluate outcomes as gains or losses relative to a reference point, tending to avoid risk in the gain domain and seek risk in the loss domain.

Decision-making process

External policy, service accessibility, costs, family support, disease perception, health literacy, self-efficacy, and prior experience interact to shape belief structures and risk preferences, which in turn influence screening intention, hospital level, screening method, and timing.
